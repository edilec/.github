# Community code of conduct

Edilec's public repositories are professional spaces for discussing software,
documentation, security, and project maintenance. Participation is welcome
when it helps people collaborate safely and evaluate ideas on their merits.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:

- communicate clearly and respectfully, including during technical disagreement;
- critique proposals and implementations without attacking the person behind them;
- make room for questions, corrections, and differing levels of experience;
- respect privacy, attribution, licences, and responsible-disclosure boundaries;
- avoid derailing project work with repeated off-topic demands; and
- follow maintainer instructions intended to protect users or keep a discussion productive.

Harassment, threats, discriminatory abuse, sexualized attention, deliberate
misgendering, doxxing, impersonation, spam, and publication of private or
security-sensitive information are not acceptable.

## Where this applies

This policy applies in Edilec-controlled repository discussions, issues, pull
requests, reviews, releases, and other project spaces. It also applies when a
participant is representing an Edilec project in a related public exchange.

## Reporting conduct concerns

Do not open a public issue about a conduct concern. Email
[hello@edilec.com](mailto:hello@edilec.com) with the subject **Community
conduct** and include the repository, relevant links, a concise description,
and any context needed to understand the report. Share only information you
are permitted to disclose.

Reports are reviewed by a maintainer who is not the subject of the concern. If
the named maintainer is involved, the matter must be escalated to an
uninvolved Edilec account owner or company lead. Access to a report will be
limited to the people needed to assess and resolve it, but absolute
confidentiality cannot be guaranteed where safety or law requires action.

## Response

Maintainers may clarify expectations, edit or remove content, issue a warning,
limit participation, close a discussion, or block an account. The response
will be proportionate to the behavior, its impact, prior incidents, and any
immediate safety or security risk.

Good-faith technical disagreement is not a conduct violation. Retaliation
against someone who raises a sincere concern is itself unacceptable.
